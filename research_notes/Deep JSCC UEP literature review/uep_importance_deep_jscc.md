# Unequal / importance-aware protection of learned (Deep JSCC / neural codec) latents: prior work

Scope: our contribution is ADJSCC latent quantized to 256 levels (DeepJSCC-Q style), packetized, protected with Reed-Solomon (RS) codes over a packet-erasure channel. Latent channels are ranked by leave-one-out erasure sensitivity and given tiered RS parity at a fixed total overhead. Result: about +3 dB over equal protection at 40% loss, worse below about 32% loss. ADJSCC attention gates do not predict erasure sensitivity; latent energy and a small learned predictor do.

Search date: 2026-11-06. Every paper below was checked against an arXiv abstract page, publisher page or indexed abstract, unless it is marked otherwise. Many 2025-2026 items are arXiv preprints. Where only the abstract was read, details beyond the abstract are listed as gaps.

## Has anyone already done importance-ranked UEP with block codes (especially RS or erasure codes) on Deep JSCC latents over packet-erasure channels?

### Takeaway
I found no paper that does exactly our combination: an image-reconstruction Deep JSCC latent (ADJSCC), quantized, with per-channel erasure-sensitivity ranking, tiered RS parity at a fixed overhead, over packet erasures. The closest work is Grad-FEC (Uyanik, Yeganli, Bajić, ICIP 2023). It ranks feature packets by importance and protects them with systematic RS erasure codes under i.i.d. packet loss. But it works on split-computing features for classification, not JSCC reconstruction, and it uses a drop-or-protect split instead of tiered parity. It is the main paper we must cite and set ourselves apart from. Other close works use repetition, polar/LDPC, rateless or power-based UEP, or bit-error channels, or handle packet loss with no FEC at all.

### Cited Findings

**Closest works (close / near-close)**

- **Grad-FEC (CLOSE, the biggest novelty threat).** K. Uyanik, S. F. Yeganli, I. V. Bajić, "Grad-FEC: Unequal Loss Protection of Deep Features in Collaborative Intelligence," IEEE ICIP 2023 (arXiv:2307.01846, July 2023). An edge front-end (ResNet-50 split at conv2_block1_add) produces a 56x56x256 tensor, quantized to 8 bits. Each packet is r = 7 consecutive rows of one tensor channel, so 8 packets per channel. Packet importance comes from a modified Grad-CAM map (gradients from the back-end, not deployable at the edge). A deployable proxy CNN is trained with MSE to imitate that map. Protection uses **systematic Reed-Solomon (n,k) erasure codes across packets**. The scheme FEC_A_B drops the A% least important packets and spends the freed slots on RS parity for the remaining B%. The channel is **i.i.d. packet loss**, P_L from 0.1 to 1, and the metric is ImageNet Top-1 on 882 images from 10 classes. Reported: "near-lossless performance even with 50% packet loss" with FEC_50_50. Validation of importance against real damage is partial: they compare dropping the most versus the least important packets, not a per-packet leave-one-out measurement. My fetch summary also says the reported results do not include an equal-overhead equal-protection FEC baseline (not double-checked in the PDF). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01846); [HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.01846)
  - Differences from ours: (i) split-computing classification features, not a JSCC image-reconstruction latent; (ii) importance from Grad-CAM of the task loss, while ours is leave-one-out erasure sensitivity on reconstruction PSNR, plus energy and a small predictor; (iii) binary drop/protect at fixed bandwidth, while ours is multi-tier RS parity at fixed overhead; (iv) no comparison of attention-based importance; (v) no crossover analysis (UEP worse than EEP at low loss).
  - Related thesis: K. Uyanik, "Unequal Loss Protection for Collaborative Intelligence," M.A.Sc. thesis, Simon Fraser University (2024 deposit). The thesis says there had been no prior research on ULP for collaborative intelligence. — [SFU Summit PDF](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2024-06/etd23028.pdf)
  - Background survey from the same group: I. V. Bajić, W. Lin, Y. Tian, "Collaborative Intelligence: Challenges and Opportunities" (arXiv:2102.06841, ICASSP 2021). It names the open problem that the error resilience of intermediate features is "largely unexplored". It suggests that attention could measure feature importance and that varying-strength channel codes or unequal power could do the protection. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.06841)

- **UEP for Digital Semantic Communication with Channel Coding (CLOSE on the UEP-for-JSCC-latent axis, distant on the channel).** S. Kim, Y. Oh, Y. Kim, N. Lee, Y.-S. Jeon, "Unequal Error Protection for Digital Semantic Communication with Channel Coding" (also titled "Channel Coding for Unequal Error Protection in Digital Semantic Communication"), arXiv:2508.03381 (v1 Aug 2025, v3 Sep 2026; preprint). Encoder: a DeepJSCC-style CNN autoencoder (after Bourtsoulatze et al.) with each feature quantized to 8 bits, on MNIST, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. Importance: **learned per-bit flip probabilities**, trained over parallel BSCs, used as target protection levels. The paper reports that these levels differ by orders of magnitude. Mechanisms: bit-level UEP by **repetition coding**, and block-level UEP that groups bits with similar targets into short blocks coded with **5G polar (128/256) or LDPC (512/1024)** codes, using finite-blocklength analysis and a closed-form threshold for when partitioning helps. Channel: **Rayleigh block fading + AWGN at 0 dB, no packet erasure.** Block-UEP beats equal-protection LDPC/polar at the same blocklength. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03381); [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03381v2)
  - Differences: bit errors, not packet erasures; importance is learned bit-flip tolerance, not measured erasure sensitivity; repetition/polar/LDPC instead of RS across packets; no attention-versus-energy analysis. It is the strongest "UEP on quantized Deep JSCC latents" paper, but on a different channel.

- **TokCom-UEP (RELATED / close on erasure + rateless UEP).** K. Zhang, Z. Jin, Z. Cheng, M. Zeng, L. Qiao, Z. Fei, "TokCom-UEP: Semantic Importance-Matched Unequal Error Protection for Resilient Image Transmission," arXiv:2511.22859 (Nov 2025, preprint). Token communication (LMM / 1D image tokenizer). Mechanism: **rateless UEP with expanding windows**, where windows holding critical tokens get higher selection probability. Channel: an erasure channel (model not given in the abstract). Beats EEP on three semantic metrics and on spectral efficiency at low overhead. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22859)
  - Differences: discrete tokenizer tokens, not a Deep JSCC continuous latent; fountain-style rateless code, not RS; the importance measure is not stated in the abstract.

- **Semantic-Aware Neural Video Codec for Error-Resilient Low-Latency Transmission (RELATED).** M. Mortaheb, H. Esfahanizadeh, J. Du, H. Viswanathan (Nokia Bell Labs), arXiv:2609.16279 (Sep 2026, preprint). Built on DCVC-RT. Encoded representations are split into packets of differing "semantic and latent-feature importance" and sent on separate priority streams. An error-resilient entropy model removes inter-packet dependencies. Trained end to end over a **multi-level packet erasure channel**. The abstract describes no FEC; priority is given by the channel's erasure levels. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16279)
- **Towards Robust Semantic Video Transmission over Block Erasure Channels (RELATED).** N. Fayaz, H. Esfahanizadeh, M. Mortaheb, J. Du, H. Viswanathan, IEEE VTC-Fall 2026 (arXiv:2607.07823). Neural JSCC for video over block-erasure channels, with spatial blocks under uniform or two-level semantic-guided non-uniform erasure, and partitioned latent features for recovery. No FEC is mentioned in the abstract. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07823)

- **RL-learned UEP for quantized semantic embeddings (RELATED).** M. T. Singh, A. Arif, "Reinforcement-Learned Unequal Error Protection for Quantized Semantic Embeddings," arXiv:2601.00186 (Jan 2026). The arXiv page lists a journal reference in *Array* (DOI 10.1016/j.array.2026.100727; the page shows the year as "2006", which looks like a typo). An RL agent sets **per-dimension adaptive repetition coding** for quantized text embeddings, guided by a composite semantic-distortion metric (embedding similarity + entity preservation). Reports +6.8% chrF and +9.3% entity preservation over uniform protection at 1 dB SNR. It argues that block codes (LDPC/RS) cannot give per-dimension protection. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00186)

- **Importance-ordered restructuring for UEP (RELATED).** X. Zhan, J. Cao, X. Zhu, N. Pappas, Z. Qin, S. Feng, "Toward Robust Semantic Communications: Proactive Importance-Ordered Restructuring for Enhanced Unequal Error Protection," arXiv:2604.00595 (Apr 2026). ISFR trains features to have a descending importance order (decreasing retention probability, increasing distortion), then jointly optimizes channel matching, feature selection, modulation and power. The protection is physical-layer resource allocation, not FEC. The abstract also says existing UEP methods assess importance "passively". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00595)

- **Generative Feature Imputing (RELATED).** J. Huang, Q. Zeng, H. Du, K. Huang, "Generative Feature Imputing — A Technique for Error-resilient Semantic Communication," arXiv:2508.17957 (Aug 2025). Packetization that groups feature elements by channel mapping, diffusion-based imputation of lost features, and **semantic-aware power allocation** as UEP by packet importance. Block fading. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17957)

- **D²-JSCC (RELATED).** J. Huang, K. Yuan, C. Huang, K. Huang, "D²-JSCC: Digital Deep Joint Source-channel Coding for Semantic Communications," arXiv:2403.07338 (Mar 2024; a ResearchGate entry dated 2025 suggests a journal version, venue not verified). Digital deep source coding with an adaptive density model plus digital channel coding. Source and channel rates are allocated by modeling E2E distortion (Bayesian model, Lipschitz bound) for a given SNR. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07338)

**Packet-loss robustness without importance-ranked FEC (DISTANT to RELATED)**

- **Every Packet Counts (RELATED, and it supports our energy finding).** Y. Wei, C. Zhou, Y. Shi, J. Wang, G. Lu, "Every Packet Counts: Dispersing Information for Loss-Resilient Learned Image Compression," ACM MM 2026 (arXiv:2608.11096). Learned image compression under packet loss. Inter-Channel Redistribution and strided Interleaved Channel Grouping spread energy and information across packets. A two-layer dual-branch autoregressive entropy model limits error cascades. +1.84 dB over LossResilientLIC at 20% loss; generalizes to Gilbert-Elliott bursts. Search snippets of the full text say that channel-dimension packet loss gives very inconsistent PSNR drops, attributed to imbalanced energy across latent channels, and that losing one high-energy channel can cause complete decoding failure. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11096); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11096)
  - Relation: they *equalize* channel importance (interleaving) instead of *protecting* it unequally, which is the opposite design choice to ours. They use no FEC (per the abstract).
- **ResiGLC.** S. Yao, J. Dai, S. Wang, G. Lu, K. Niu, W. Xu, W. Zhang, P. Zhang, "Loss-Resilient Semantic Communication over Packet-Loss Networks at Extreme-Low Bandwidth," to appear in IEEE TMC (arXiv:2608.19590, Aug 2026). Generative latent coding with masked in-context latent prediction and progressive decoding. No FEC or importance ranking in the abstract. DISTANT. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19590)
- **LGSemCom.** "Lightweight Generative Image Semantic Communication over Packet Erasure Channels," arXiv:2609.06989 (2026). Generative recovery of erased regions with erasure-aware weighting. It cites ResiComp (masked-token packet-loss concealment) as prior packet-level JSCC. DISTANT (concealment, not FEC). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06989)
- **DeepJSCC-l / bandwidth-agile.** D. B. Kurka, D. Gündüz, "Bandwidth-Agile Image Transmission with Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding," IEEE TWC 2021. Layered latent blocks trained with block "structured dropout" (zeros for blocks not received), which gives an implicit importance ordering of blocks. DISTANT (analog, no FEC). — [Imperial PDF](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/research-centres-and-groups/ipc-lab/layered_JSCC_journal.pdf)
- **Rateless JSCC.** "Rateless Joint Source-Channel Coding, and a Blueprint for 6G Semantic Communications System Design," arXiv:2502.06095 (2025). Binary latents trained sequentially so importance falls monotonically. Its comparison table marks frame-level packet erasure as not supported. DISTANT. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06095)
- **Deep JSCC of text over erasure channels.** N. Farsad, M. Rao, A. Goldsmith, "Deep Learning for Joint Source-Channel Coding of Text," ICASSP 2018 (arXiv:1802.06832). The separate-coding baseline assumes an RS code able to correct the expected erasures; no UEP. DISTANT. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.06832)

### Inferences
- Closeness ranking (most to least threatening):
  1. Grad-FEC (2023): **close**. The same machinery (8-bit features, channel-row packets, systematic RS erasure codes across packets, importance-ranked, i.i.d. packet loss) applied to split-computing classification. We must cite it and frame our work as the reconstruction-JSCC counterpart, with (a) a ground-truth leave-one-out sensitivity measure, (b) multi-tier parity at fixed overhead against an equal-protection baseline, (c) an explicit loss-rate crossover where UEP loses, and (d) evidence that attention is a poor importance proxy.
  2. Kim et al. arXiv:2508.03381 (2025/2026): **close** on "UEP of quantized DeepJSCC latents with block codes", **distant** on channel (bit errors / fading, polar/LDPC/repetition, no erasures).
  3. TokCom-UEP (2025), Mortaheb et al. (2026), Fayaz et al. (2026), Singh & Arif (2026), Zhan et al. (2026), Huang et al. 2508.17957 (2025): **related**.
  4. Every Packet Counts (2026): **related**, with a complementary, opposite design choice (dispersal versus UEP) and independent support for the latent-energy finding.
  5. ResiGLC, LGSemCom, DeepJSCC-l, rateless JSCC, D²-JSCC: **distant**.
- None is a near-duplicate. The combination of ADJSCC + DeepJSCC-Q-style 256-level quantization + RS packet-erasure UEP + an attention-versus-energy-versus-learned importance study still appears new as of Nov 2026. The novelty is incremental relative to Grad-FEC and classical ULP, though, so the paper should present its empirical findings as the contribution: the crossover, the failure of attention as a proxy, and energy as a cheap predictor.
- The 2026 trend (Every Packet Counts dispersing energy; Nokia's priority streams) means a reviewer may ask whether interleaving or dispersal beats UEP at low loss. Our observed crossover near 32% fits that view and could be framed against it.

## What importance measures have been used for latent features in semantic communication (attention, gradients, energy, learned importance, task-based importance), and were they validated against actual erasure damage?

### Takeaway
Measures used so far: Grad-CAM / gradient maps (Grad-FEC), ViT attention scores (Park et al.), learned bit-flip tolerance (Kim et al.), RL-learned per-dimension allocation (Singh & Arif), pre-trained language model scores (Guo et al.), bottleneck attribution (Entropy 2025 MIMO paper), task-relevance/RL (Wang et al. TCCN 2024), and enforced ordering (ISFR, DeepJSCC-l). Hardly any validate the measure against measured per-unit erasure damage. Grad-FEC does a coarse most-versus-least-important drop test. "Every Packet Counts" observes that latent-channel energy imbalance drives packet-loss damage but uses it to motivate dispersal, not ranking. None of the verified papers tests whether ADJSCC-style SNR attention gates predict erasure sensitivity.

### Cited Findings
- **Gradient / Grad-CAM:** Grad-FEC uses a modified Grad-CAM at the split point (no channel GAP or summation). Packet score = sum of the map over the packet's elements. A deployable proxy network regresses that map from the input. Validation: dropping most versus least important packets; accuracy holds "even with 60% lost packets" when only the least important are lost; the proxy is "not as good" as Grad-CAM. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.01846)
- **Attention (ViT):** J. Park, Y. Oh, Y. Kim, Y.-S. Jeon, "Vision Transformer-based Semantic Communications With Importance-Aware Quantization," arXiv:2412.06038 (Dec 2024; listed as IEEE IoT J. 2025 in 2508.03381's references). Pretrained ViT attention scores serve as patch importance. More quantization bits go to important patches (incremental optimal and water-filling allocation), with an extension to an equivalent BSC. Classification task. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06038)
- **Attention (ViT) + MIMO-OFDM resources:** J. Park, Y. Oh, Y.-S. Jeon, "Importance-Aware Semantic Communication in MIMO-OFDM Systems Using Vision Transformer," arXiv:2508.07696 (2025). Joint quantization, subcarrier mapping and power allocation from ViT importance (block coordinate descent, finite-blocklength extension). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07696)
- **Attention for collaborative inference:** J. Im et al., "Attention-aware semantic communications for collaborative inference," IEEE IoT J. 2024. Cited in 2508.03381; not independently verified. — [arXiv 2508.03381 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03381v2)
- **Learned bit-level reliability:** Kim et al. learn per-bit flip probabilities over parallel BSCs and use them as target protection levels. These differ by orders of magnitude across bits. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03381)
- **RL with a semantic distortion metric:** Singh & Arif. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00186)
- **Language-model importance:** S. Guo, Y. Wang, S. Li, N. Saeed, "Semantic Importance-Aware Communications Using Pre-trained Language Models," IEEE Communications Letters 2023 (DOI 10.1109/LCOMM.2023.3293805; arXiv:2302.07142). ChatGPT or BERT scores importance per frame or word, and a cross-layer manager allocates power. v1 framed this as word-ordered UEP. Follow-up: "Semantic Importance-Aware Communications with Semantic Correction Using Large Language Models," arXiv:2405.16011 (2024). — [arXiv 2302.07142](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.07142v2); [arXiv 2405.16011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16011v1)
- **Bottleneck attribution:** "Importance-Aware Resource Allocations for MIMO Semantic Communication," Entropy 27(6):605, 2025 (MDPI; authors not recorded in my notes). A VAE encoder, a "communication-informed bottleneck attribution" importance measure, and WMMSE-based importance-aware power allocation. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/27/6/605); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12191451/)
- **Task relevance with RL:** Y. Wang, S. Han, X. Xu, H. Liang, R. Meng, C. Dong, P. Zhang, "Feature Importance-Aware Task-Oriented Semantic Transmission and Optimization," IEEE TCCN 2024 (DOI 10.1109/TCCN.2024.3375496). Sends only task-relevant features and jointly optimizes channel assignment and feature selection for a TOSSE metric with knowledge-assisted PPO. Verified via indexed abstract (AMiner). — [AMiner](https://www.aminer.org/pub/65f3c40113fb2c6cf67a66f1/feature-importance-aware-task-oriented-semantic-transmission-and-optimization)
- **Feature-importance-aware DJSCC for computation:** "Feature Importance-Aware Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding for Computationally Efficient and Adjustable Image Transmission," arXiv:2504.04758 (2025). Importance ratio γ picks the top features for deformable self-attention processing. The importance here serves computation, not protection. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04758v2)
- **Enforced ordering instead of measured importance:** ISFR (Zhan et al. 2026) [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00595); DeepJSCC-l structured block dropout [Imperial PDF](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/research-centres-and-groups/ipc-lab/layered_JSCC_journal.pdf); rateless JSCC monotone importance via sequential training [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06095).
- **Energy:** "Every Packet Counts" links inconsistent PSNR drops under channel-wise packet loss to imbalanced latent-channel energy and reports that losing one high-energy channel can cause decoding failure (from search snippets of the HTML). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11096)
- **ADJSCC attention:** J. Xu, B. Ai, W. Chen, A. Yang, P. Sun, M. Rodrigues, "Wireless Image Transmission Using Deep Source Channel Coding With Attention Modules," IEEE TCSVT 2022 (arXiv:2012.00533). Its attention-feature modules combine global feature statistics with SNR to produce channel scaling factors. These are learned for AWGN SNR adaptation, not for erasure importance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.00533)

### Inferences
- Our finding that ADJSCC attention gates do *not* predict erasure sensitivity, while latent energy does, has no direct precedent that I found. Bajić et al.'s survey proposed attention as an importance measure, and Park et al. use ViT attention, so the negative result is relevant and citable against them. Our leave-one-out erasure sensitivity is a stronger ground truth than Grad-FEC's drop test.
- The energy result agrees with "Every Packet Counts" (2026). We should cite it as independent evidence and note that the conclusions differ: they equalize energy, we exploit it.

### Gaps
- No verified paper reports the rank correlation between any importance proxy and measured per-channel erasure damage for a Deep JSCC latent. That absence is itself the finding, but full-text checks of the ViT and ISFR papers were not done.
- Author list for the Entropy 2025 MIMO paper was not captured.

## Are there works on "semantic importance aware" power allocation, adaptive modulation, or channel coding for semantic communication (e.g., by Xie, Qin, Gündüz, Kurka, Tung, Bourtsoulatze, Yang, Dai, Niu, Zhang)?

### Takeaway
Yes, many, mostly on power, subcarrier, bit or modulation allocation over AWGN/fading channels: Guo et al. 2023; Park/Oh/Jeon 2024-2025; Wang/Zhang TCCN 2024; Zhan/Qin 2026; Huang/Huang 2025; importance-aware constellations 2026. Most of the Gündüz/Kurka/Tung/Bourtsoulatze work gives implicit importance (DeepJSCC-l layering, DeepJSCC-Q constellation constraints) rather than explicit UEP. The Dai/Niu/Zhang group's 2026 packet-loss work (ResiGLC) uses generative resilience, not FEC. None of these is RS/packet-erasure based.

These are implicit, learned importance-aware power allocation in analog form.
- **Dai / Niu / Zhang group:** ResiGLC (IEEE TMC, 2026) for packet loss via generative latent modeling. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19590)

### Inferences
- The "semantic importance-aware" literature works almost entirely at the PHY level (power, subcarriers, constellation, quantization bits) over AWGN or fading channels. Application-layer packet-erasure FEC on Deep JSCC latents is underexplored, and that is where our work sits.

### Gaps

## Are there works on UEP for learned image compression bitstreams (e.g., hyperprior/entropy-coded neural codecs with UEP) or for split computing / feature transmission over lossy links?

### Takeaway
Split computing: yes. Grad-FEC (ICIP 2023) and the SFU thesis are directly importance-ranked RS-based ULP of deep features over packet loss. Learned image compression: the 2026 loss-resilience work (Every Packet Counts, ACM MM 2026; LossResilientLIC as its baseline; ResiGLC) disperses information or conceals losses instead of applying UEP. I found no verified paper applying importance-tiered FEC to a hyperprior/entropy-coded neural image codec bitstream. The nearest is the Nokia DCVC-RT video work, which uses priority streams with a multi-level erasure channel.

### Cited Findings
- Grad-FEC (ICIP 2023) and the Uyanik SFU thesis: ULP for collaborative intelligence with RS erasure codes. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01846); [thesis](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2024-06/etd23028.pdf)
- "Latent-Space Inpainting for Packet Loss Concealment in Collaborative Object Detection," arXiv:2102.00142 (Bajić group, 2021). Reports that about 5% loss drops detection accuracy by about 20% without concealment (per search snippet), which motivates recovery or protection. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.00142)
- Every Packet Counts (ACM MM 2026): loss-resilient LIC with channel interleaving and energy redistribution, no UEP. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11096)
- Mortaheb et al. (2026): DCVC-RT neural video, importance-graded packets on priority streams over a multi-level packet erasure channel. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16279)
- Classical UEP for SPIHT/JPEG-XS streams (Mohr-Riskin-Ladner JSAC 2000; JPEG-XS FEC 2022) are the non-learned predecessors. — [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/848236/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.04825v2)

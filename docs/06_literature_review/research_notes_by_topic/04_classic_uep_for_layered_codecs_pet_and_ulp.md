# Classic (non-learned) Unequal Error Protection for Scalable / Progressive / Layered Codecs over Packet-Erasure Channels

Scope note: the bibliographic details below (authors, title, venue, volume/pages, DOI) were checked against the paper page, IEEE metadata mirrors, institutional repositories, or DBLP. Unless a finding says otherwise, method summaries come from abstracts and repository records, not full texts. "DOI unverified" marks a DOI I could not confirm.

Important framing for our paper: our layout (one RS(n,k_i) codeword per byte column across the same n packets, with different k_i per tier) is the same construction as **Priority Encoding Transmission (PET)** / **Unequal Loss Protection (ULP)**. Reviewers will spot this, so the paper should say so directly and cite PET (Albanese et al. 1996) and ULP (Mohr, Riskin, Ladner 2000) as the origin of the layout.

## Q1. Priority Encoding Transmission (PET) and its applications to progressive images (Mohr/Riskin/Ladner ULP; Puri & Ramchandran MD-FEC; Stockhammer)

### Takeaway
PET (Albanese, Blömer, Edmonds, Luby, Sudan; FOCS 1994 and IEEE Trans. IT 1996) is the founding work. Each message segment is assigned a "priority", the fraction of received packets needed to recover it, and is erasure-coded across all packets. The paper proves that no system can implement a set of priorities at total rate above one. Mohr-Riskin-Ladner (ULP, JSAC 2000) and Puri-Ramchandran (MD-FEC, Asilomar 1999) applied PET-style cross-packet RS coding to SPIHT and other progressive bitstreams, which yields graceful degradation instead of a cliff. This is the direct ancestor of our tiered RS layout.

### Inferences
- Our "one RS codeword per column across packets, different k per tier" layout is structurally PET/ULP. The new elements are (i) the source: a learned JSCC latent with no natural embedded ordering, and (ii) the importance ranking, which is learned or measured per channel instead of read off the bitstream's prefix order.
- PET's "rate ≤ 1" bound is the formal reason tiering cannot be free. At a fixed 50% overhead, giving one tier more parity must take parity from another. Below the EEP threshold the weaker tier then fails earlier than EEP would. This is the trade-off we observe empirically.
- From memory (not verified against the PDF text, which could not be rendered): PET's capacity condition is usually written as a "girth" sum, Σ_i (m_i / ρ_i) ≤ packet payload, where m_i is the size of segment i and ρ_i its priority fraction. Check this in the journal PDF before quoting it.

### Gaps
- I could not render the PET journal PDF, so the exact girth formula, the specific erasure code used (Cauchy/RS-style MDS), and whether the paper shows an MPEG demo are unverified.
- I did not read the full ULP JSAC text. Its exact optimization algorithm (the original JSAC version vs. the ICIP 2000 approximation) and its stated complexity are known only at abstract level.
- I found no Stockhammer paper that applies PET to progressive images specifically. The Stockhammer work I verified (TCSVT 2002) uses RCPC/FEED, not PET.

## Q2. Optimal UEP / FEC allocation for embedded bitstreams over packet-erasure (and BSC) channels, and its complexity

### Takeaway
The classic optimizers are: Sherwood & Zeger (1997, BSC, RCPC+CRC for SPIHT); Chande & Farvardin (JSAC 2000, BSC and packet-erasure, dynamic programming that maximizes the expected useful source rate); Mohr-Riskin-Ladner (ULP, convex-hull-based approximation); Stankovic-Hamzaoui-Saupe/Xiong (fast rate-based optimum and linear-time local search toward distortion-based optimum); and Dumitrescu-Wu-Wang (globally optimal in O(N²L²), or O(NL²) under convexity). Hamzaoui-Stankovic-Xiong (IEEE SPM 2005) surveys this whole line.

### Cited Findings
- **Sherwood, P. G.; Zeger, K., "Progressive image coding for noisy channels," IEEE Signal Processing Letters, vol. 4, no. 7, pp. 189–191, July 1997. DOI 10.1109/97.596882.** Cascades an existing progressive image coder (SPIHT) with carefully chosen error-control coding (the full text describes RCPC with CRC). For Lena 512×512 at 1 bpp on a BSC, it beats prior results by at least 2.6, 2.8, and 8.9 dB at BER 1e-3, 1e-2, and 1e-1. A follow-up appeared in IEEE Trans. Commun. 1998 ("Error protection for progressive image transmission over memoryless and fading channels"). — [ISC mirror](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/1287039.htm); [Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/883c016de6169e65022e188553d386b4); [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/SherwoodZ97-0/bibtex)
- **Chande, V.; Farvardin, N., "Progressive transmission of images over memoryless noisy channels," IEEE JSAC, vol. 18, no. 6, pp. 850–860, June 2000. DOI 10.1109/49.848239.** JSCC for progressive transmission over memoryless bit-error **or packet-erasure** channels, using embedded source codes and embedded error-correcting/detecting channel codes. It gives a design algorithm for UEP at a target rate, evaluated by average distortion, average PSNR, and average useful source rate. Policies that maximize the average useful source rate also give optimal UEP at several intermediate rates (progressive optimality). — [Stevens record](https://researchwith.stevens.edu/en/publications/progressive-transmission-of-images-over-memoryless-noisy-channels/); [ISC mirror](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/1350355.htm); [Chande PhD thesis, UMD](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/items/8b4baf91-484c-4948-8d83-c725942c398e)
- **Stankovic, V.; Hamzaoui, R.; Saupe, D., "Fast algorithm for rate-based optimal error protection of embedded codes," IEEE Trans. Communications, vol. 51, no. 11, pp. 1788–1795, 2003. DOI 10.1109/TCOMM.2003.819235.** Speeds up Chande-Farvardin's rate-based optimum (which maximizes the expected number of correctly received source bits). Key observation: an optimal strategy protects successive source blocks with the same code, which gives an explicit formula for the number of such blocks. Reported speed-ups are 2.82–44.76× at 0.25–2 bpp on a BSC. — [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/5190/); [KOPS Konstanz](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/21756d53-92b3-4ad4-be26-1ebe48d7aade)
- **Stankovic, V.; Hamzaoui, R.; Xiong, Z., "Real-time error protection of embedded codes for packet erasure and fading channels," IEEE TCSVT, vol. 14, no. 8, pp. 1064–1072, 2004.** For packet-erasure channels it uses systematic RS codes across packets (a PET-style product layout). A low-memory, linear-time iterative improvement algorithm computes the protection, and on 2-D and 3-D SPIHT it comes close to optimal average PSNR at lower run time than earlier methods. DOI unverified. — [Strathclyde PurePortal](https://pureportal.strath.ac.uk/en/publications/real-time-error-protection-of-embedded-codes-for-packet-erasure-a/); [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/5192/)
  - Related point: a rate-based optimal solution can be found in linear time, but computing a distortion-based optimum is "prohibitive". The authors propose a local search that starts from the rate-based optimum and converges in linear time to a local minimum of expected distortion. — [KOPS Konstanz preprint record](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/304a5cdc-2daf-4609-9ae6-a4368113b08e)
  - Related: "Product code error protection of packetized multimedia bitstreams" (ICIP 2003), a linear-time optimal EEP for a product-code system. — [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/38564/)
  - Related: Hamzaoui, Stankovic, Xiong, "Fast algorithm for distortion-based error protection of embedded image codes," IEEE TIP 2005. — [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/12848/)
- **Dumitrescu, S.; Wu, X.; Wang, Z., "Globally optimal uneven error-protected packetization of scalable code streams," IEEE Trans. Multimedia, vol. 6, no. 2, pp. 230–239, Apr. 2004. DOI 10.1109/TMM.2003.822793.** Rate-fidelity-optimal UEP packetization (PET/ULP-style layout). With no assumption on the loss PMF or the rate-fidelity function, it finds the global optimum in O(N²L²), against a previously claimed O(N³L²), where N is the number of packets and L the payload size. It drops to O(NL²) if the rate-fidelity function is convex and the loss PMF is monotonically decreasing, and it avoids fractional bit allocation. A DCC 2002 version (DOI 10.1109/DCC.2002.999945) came first. — [author PDF](https://www.ece.mcmaster.ca/~sorina/papers/UEP_TMM_last.pdf); [McMaster record](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/146174); [ISC mirror](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/930416.htm)
- **Hamzaoui, R.; Stankovic, V.; Xiong, Z., "Optimized error protection of scalable image bit streams [Advances in joint source-channel coding for images]," IEEE Signal Processing Magazine, vol. 22, no. 6, pp. 91–107, Nov. 2005. DOI 10.1109/MSP.2005.1550192.** Survey of FEC for scalable image coders across several channel models, with emphasis on UEP source-channel bit allocation. It notes that most of these systems extend to scalable video and 3-D meshes. **Best single "classic UEP" citation for reviewers.** — [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/36506/)
- **Thomos, N.; Boulgouris, N. V.; Strintzis, M. G., "Wireless image transmission using turbo codes and optimal unequal error protection," IEEE TIP, vol. 14, no. 11, 2005** (SPIHT), and **"Optimized transmission of JPEG2000 streams over wireless channels," IEEE TIP, vol. 15, no. 1, pp. 54–67, Jan. 2006. DOI 10.1109/TIP.2005.860338.** The 2006 paper reorganizes JPEG2000 streams into error-resilient product-coded packets (turbo codes within packets, RS across packets), optimized iteratively. — [EPFL PDF (TIP 2006)](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/149305/files/TIP-01-2006-JPEG2000.pdf); [catalog record (TIP 2005)](https://katalog.dhi-paris.fr/vufind/Record/NLM158853784/Description)

### Inferences
- Everything in this line assumes an **embedded (prefix-decodable) bitstream**: the utility of segment i counts only if segments 1..i-1 are decoded, so the optimal protection is monotonically non-increasing (more parity on earlier segments). Our latent has no prefix dependency, since each channel is independently useful. Utility is therefore additive across tiers, not prefix-conditional. This makes the allocation problem easier (a separable knapsack-like problem over tiers) and removes the monotonicity constraint. It is a real, citable difference.
- Our setup is coarse (2–3 tiers, a fixed 50% overhead, and a fixed number of packets), so the classic optimizers are overkill. However, reviewers may ask why we did not run a Mohr-style or Dumitrescu-style optimizer over the k_i. An exhaustive search over (k_1, k_2, k_3) at fixed total parity is trivially cheap, and the paper could report it as the "optimal UEP allocation" baseline, citing Dumitrescu et al. for global optimality.

### Gaps
- I did not read full texts, so the exact complexity of Chande-Farvardin's DP and of the original Mohr ULP algorithm is not confirmed here. The O(N³L²) "previously claimed" figure that Dumitrescu et al. cite likely refers to one of these, but the abstract does not say which.

## Q3. UEP for JPEG2000 (JPWL), H.264/SVC with Raptor/fountain codes, UEP-LT, and expanding-window fountain codes

### Takeaway
JPWL (JPEG 2000 Part 11) standardizes RS-based Error Protection Blocks with UEP over the codestream. For SVC, Raptor-based FEC (DVB-H/3GPP) and layer-aware FEC (LA-FEC, Hellge et al.) are the standard references. On the rateless side, UEP-LT/Raptor (Rahnavard, Vellambi, Fekri, IT 2007) uses weighted (non-uniform) selection of input symbols, and Expanding Window Fountain codes (Sejdinovic et al., TCOM 2009) use nested windows. Both are designed for importance classes.

### Inferences
- Reviewers may ask why we use RS rather than Raptor or EWF. Defensible reasons: (a) a short block (number of packets per image is small) where RS is exactly MDS while LT/Raptor carry reception overhead at small k; (b) GF(256) bytes match our byte-quantized latent directly; (c) a fixed rate (no rateless need). Rahnavard et al.'s finite-length bounds are the citation for the claim that rateless UEP has finite-length overhead.
- LA-FEC is the closest classic analogue to "tiering should not lose efficiency below the cliff". Because enhancement-layer parity also covers the base layer, it reduces the EEP-vs-UEP penalty our results show. It is a natural related-work and future-work pointer (a nested/expanding-window RS or LA-FEC layout over our tiers).

- I did not verify the exact JPWL standard citation (ISO/IEC 15444-11:2007). It is cited from memory and should be confirmed before use.
- I found no single paper giving a canonical "SVC + UEP Raptor" baseline. Schierl et al. 2007's UEP content is unconfirmed, and LA-FEC (2011) is the best verified one.

## Q4. Block-length effects: why splitting into smaller RS blocks loses efficiency at low loss (finite-length / MDS behaviour), and the "cliff effect"

### Takeaway
For an MDS (n,k) code over a packet-erasure channel, decoding succeeds exactly when at most n−k packets are lost. Residual failure is therefore a binomial (or Gilbert) tail, and it gets steeper (more cliff-like) as n grows. Classic packet-FEC analyses (Frossard 2001; EPFL 2008 short-block work; McCann & Fendick) and finite-blocklength theory (Polyanskiy-Poor-Verdú 2010, with an explicit BEC treatment) give the formal basis. I found no classic UEP paper that analyzes "UEP vs. one big EEP block below the cliff" as a block-length effect specifically. That framing has to be derived by us.

### Cited Findings
- With MDS codes, if more than n−k symbols of a block are lost, none of the lost source symbols can be recovered. When the loss rate hovers near the redundancy level, residual losses cluster in certain blocks. The authors propose short block codes and partial RS (protecting only part of the source) to reduce this fluctuation, which helps error concealment in video. — [EPFL LTS4, ICME 2008](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/lts4/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/icme2008b.pdf)
- Frossard (IEEE Communications Letters 2001, "FEC performance in multimedia streaming") computes the post-FEC video packet loss rate and the average burst length under a Gilbert loss model. — [EPFL LTS4 PDF](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/lts4/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/comml01.pdf)
- McCann & Fendick give an analytical model of residual-loss run lengths for (N+K, K) RS codes as a function of the network loss rate, for a single block and for block sequences. — [arXiv 1911.03265](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.03265)
- **Polyanskiy, Y.; Poor, H. V.; Verdú, S., "Channel coding rate in the finite blocklength regime," IEEE Trans. IT, vol. 56, no. 5, pp. 2307–2359, May 2010.** The maximal rate at blocklength n and error ε is approximately C − √(V/n)·Q⁻¹(ε). The paper treats the BEC explicitly, with upper and lower bounds tight to within a few bits. DOI not checked in this search (believed to be 10.1109/TIT.2010.2043769). — [PDF](https://www.math.chalmers.se/~hegarty/finite_block.pdf)
- Rahnavard et al. 2007 give finite-length ML error bounds for LT/Raptor under EEP and UEP (see Q3). — [Georgia Tech handle](https://hdl.handle.net/1853/26673)
- Horn et al. 1999 model Internet packet loss as a two-state Markov (Gilbert) process. — [datalearner record](https://www.datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/0923-5965/volumes-and-issues/57/paper-detail/102876)

### Inferences (derivations by us, not stated in the sources above)
- **Our cliff location follows directly from MDS behavior.** 50% total overhead means n = 1.5k, so an EEP code tolerates (n−k)/n = 1/3 of packets lost. That matches our observed cliff at about 33%. With column-interleaving across all n packets, every column codeword sees the same erasure pattern, so all columns succeed or fail together. This is a sharp cliff, smoothed only by the randomness of the number of lost packets.
- **Two different mechanisms explain "tiering loses below the cliff". The paper should say which one applies.**
  1. *Same packets, different k_i (PET layout).* Every tier spans all n packets, so block length is unchanged. The penalty is purely the PET rate-≤1 budget: a lower-priority tier must have fewer parity symbols than EEP, so its threshold drops below 1/3 and it fails in the 20–33% loss band where EEP still recovers everything. This is a parity-budget effect, not a block-length effect.
  2. *Separate packet groups per tier (smaller n per tier).* The fraction of packets lost in each group concentrates less around p (binomial variance p(1−p)/n), so for p below threshold each small block has a higher failure probability. This is the finite-length penalty, the classic "longer codes are more efficient" result (Polyanskiy-Poor-Verdú; MDS binomial tail).
- The usual "cliff effect" citation in Deep JSCC papers is the analog-vs-digital comparison (SoftCast, Bourtsoulatze et al. 2019). In the classic packet-FEC literature the equivalent is the MDS threshold. PET/ULP explicitly aimed to turn the cliff into a staircase (graceful degradation), and Mohr et al.'s "40% loss with reasonable quality when optimized for 20%" is the canonical example.

### Gaps
- I found no classic paper that explicitly quantifies "UEP vs. one large EEP RS block, below vs. above the EEP threshold" at fixed overhead. Mohr et al. and Dumitrescu et al. optimize for a loss PMF, which handles the trade-off implicitly. A head-to-head analytical treatment would need to be derived in our paper.
- I did not verify a classic source using the term "cliff effect" for packet-FEC thresholds, as opposed to analog/digital broadcast.

## Q5. Classic UEP when the source has no natural embedded ordering (importance must be estimated)

### Takeaway
The main classic precedent is non-scalable video (H.263/H.264). There, packets or slices are ranked by estimated or measured distortion-if-lost (sometimes FMO slice groups), and RS/FEC is allocated by RD optimization. Mohr's group handled H.263 by *reordering* the bitstream to make it embedded. Gallant & Kossentini built RD-optimized layering for UEP. I found no classic work that learns the importance ranking itself. That part, plus the latent being a learned channel-wise representation, is what is new in our work.

### Cited Findings
- **Gallant, M.; Kossentini, F., "Rate-distortion optimized layered coding with unequal error protection for robust Internet video," IEEE TCSVT, vol. 11, no. 3, pp. 357–372, 2001. DOI 10.1109/76.911161.** Layered coding with transport prioritization. RD-optimized mode selection within and across layers uses a JSCC approach and jointly considers packetization, concealment, and channel-code strength. — [DBLP](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/tcsv/GallantK01.html); [ISC mirror](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/1456081.htm)
- **Goshi, J.; Mohr, A. E.; Ladner, R. E.; Riskin, E. A.; Lippman, A., "Unequal loss protection for H.263 compressed video," IEEE TCSVT, 2005.** Applies ULP to non-embedded H.263 by reordering the bitstream to make it embedded. Author list beyond Goshi/Mohr/Ladner/Riskin not fully verified. — [Stevens record](https://researchwith.stevens.edu/en/publications/unequal-loss-protection-for-h263-compressed-video/)
- Shih & Tsai (NYCU, ~2010), "A new unequal error protection scheme based on FMO": slice groups of different importance get different amounts of FEC. Starting from equal allocation, FEC packets are moved between groups until distortion is minimized (greedy RD). — [NYCU repository](https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/handle/11536/5495)
- Thomos et al., ICME 2006: FMO groups macroblocks by importance, and slice-group formation and RS protection are optimized alternately. — [ICME 2006 PDF](https://www.cecs.uci.edu/~papers/icme06/pdfs/0000593.pdf)
- A 2010 Optical Engineering paper derives a distortion model to rank H.264 partitions and P-frames within a GOP by importance. The model matches simulated distortion well. — [SPIE](https://biomedicaloptics.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/optical-engineering/volume-49/issue-08/080501/Picture-group-based-priority-sorting-method-for-H264-AVC-video/10.1117/1.3479933.full)
- A preprint prioritizes H.264 slices by PSNR contribution and protects them with expanding-window rateless codes, claiming better efficiency than SVC. — [CORE preprint](https://core.ac.uk/download/293884218.pdf)

### Inferences
- The classic answer to "no embedded order" is to **measure distortion-if-lost per unit (slice, partition, frame) and then allocate**. That is methodologically the same as our "measured channel-importance ranking". To claim novelty, we should stress what is specific to learned latents: (i) the ranking is computed once per model (or learned during training), not per frame or image; (ii) channel importance in a Deep JSCC latent is additive and decoder-dependent, not driven by prediction or drift; (iii) the decoder degrades gracefully on missing channels (zero-fill) without concealment heuristics.
- Mohr's group "reorder to make embedded" is the closest conceptual precedent for sorting latent channels by importance and then applying PET. It should be cited as such.

### Gaps
- I did not find classic papers that *learn* the importance ordering, or that apply PET/ULP to transform-coefficient groups without an embedded coder. A targeted search on DCT/wavelet subband-level UEP for non-embedded JPEG may find some.
- The Shih & Tsai and Thomos ICME 2006 bibliographic details are partial (exact venue for Shih & Tsai unconfirmed).

## Q6 (Objective c). Classic baselines reviewers are likely to expect

### Takeaway
The most likely reviewer requests: (1) state that our layout *is* PET/ULP and cite it; (2) add a PET/ULP-optimal allocation baseline (exhaustive or Dumitrescu-style search over k_i for a given loss PMF), not only hand-picked tiers; (3) compare to a classic separation baseline, JPEG2000 quality layers (or SPIHT) + ULP/PET with RS at the same 50% overhead, or at least cite it; (4) possibly a fountain-based UEP (EWF or UEP-LT) or LA-FEC comparison.

### Inferences
- **What is old:** UEP itself; RS/MDS erasure coding across packets with per-segment k (PET 1996); optimal parity allocation for a loss PMF (Chande-Farvardin 2000, Mohr 2000, Stankovic/Hamzaoui 2003–2005, Dumitrescu 2004); the graceful-degradation-vs-cliff trade-off; rateless and layer-aware UEP variants.
- **What is (plausibly) new:** applying PET-style RS UEP to a *learned JSCC latent* that has no prefix/embedded structure, where tier utility is additive across channels and the importance ranking is learned or measured from the decoder. Also the empirical characterization of when tiering beats EEP at fixed overhead for such latents (only above the EEP threshold of 1/3 loss at 50% overhead). Note that the last point is a known qualitative consequence of PET's rate bound, so frame it as a characterization for learned latents, not a new phenomenon.
- **Cheapest strong addition:** report a "PET-optimal" curve, the best (k_1..k_T) per loss rate or for a loss PMF found by exhaustive search. This preempts the question of whether the tiers were chosen optimally, and shows where the hand-designed tiers sit relative to the classic optimum.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published, directly comparable classic baseline at our exact operating point (byte-level RS(GF(256)) across packets at 50% overhead, Kodak/CIFAR images). Reviewers would expect us to build the JPEG2000/SPIHT + PET baseline ourselves.

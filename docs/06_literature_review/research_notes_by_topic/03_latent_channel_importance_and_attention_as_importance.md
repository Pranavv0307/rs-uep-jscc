# Latent-channel importance in learned codecs / Deep JSCC / semantic communication, and whether attention / SE gates indicate importance

Context being judged: in ADJSCC (SNR-conditioned SE-style attention feature modules), we find that bottleneck attention gate values do NOT predict true leave-one-out (LOO) erasure damage (within-image Spearman -0.015, top-4 overlap at chance). Latent energy / L1 norm (Spearman +0.84) and a 2048-MAC learned sensitivity head (top-4 overlap 0.92) do predict it. The sorted profile is stable across seeds (0.92) while channel indices permute, and it transfers from CIFAR-10 to CIFAR-100 (0.997).

Verification note: items marked [V] had their existence and key content checked by search or fetch in this session. Items marked [K] are canonical papers with well-known arXiv IDs. They were not re-fetched this session, but their existence and the claims attributed to them are standard, high-confidence knowledge. The report writer may want to spot-check [K] links before final citation.

## Q1. Has anyone shown that attention/SE gates in JSCC do or do not reflect channel importance for erasures?

### Takeaway
I found no paper that tests whether ADJSCC-style (or any SE / channel-attention) gate values in a Deep JSCC encoder predict the real damage of erasing a latent channel. The closest work (Zhan et al. 2026) measures exactly our LOO-PSNR importance. It treats attention-module JSCC as an "implicit" importance mechanism, but it never compares gate values against LOO damage. The negative result on gates looks novel. The LOO-PSNR measurement itself does not, because it has a direct 2026 precedent.

### Cited Findings
- **Xu, Ai, Chen, Zhang, Shao, "Wireless Image Transmission Using Deep Source Channel Coding With Attention Modules," IEEE TCSVT 2022 (ADJSCC)** [V]. The attention feature (AF) module pools the feature maps globally, concatenates the SNR, and predicts a per-channel scaling factor. The stated rationale is that the network "learn[s] to assign different weights to different features for a given SNR." That rationale is an interpretive claim, and the paper does not validate it against per-channel damage. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351791034_Wireless_Image_Transmission_Using_Deep_Source_Channel_Coding_With_Attention_Modules); arXiv [K]: [2012.00533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00533). Closeness: the subject of our study. It makes the implicit claim we test and does not validate it.
- **Zhan, Cao, Zhu, Pappas, Qin, Feng, "Toward Robust Semantic Communications: Proactive Importance-Ordered Restructuring for Enhanced Unequal Error Protection," arXiv:2604.00595, Apr 2026 (ISFR)** [V]. This paper defines feature importance as the drop in task score when one feature is zeroed (Eq. 17), normalised to sum to 1 (Eq. 18). The weights are "derived from PSNR variations on the validation set." That is exactly a leave-one-out PSNR importance. Training uses modules with "decreasing retention probabilities" so that key semantics are pushed into the front-end features. UEP then jointly optimises channel matching, feature selection, modulation and power to minimise importance-weighted distortion. Fig. 4 shows that masking front features is severe and masking tail features is mild. The paper groups prior importance methods as "passive" (Grad-CAM, average gradients, feature-wise masking, ViT attention scores, learned evaluation networks). It contrasts attention-module JSCC (its ref [10]) as an "implicit, channel-adaptive approach." It does NOT evaluate SE / attention gates as importance predictors, and it does not test energy proxies. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00595), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00595v1). **Closeness: close. This is the most important paper to cite.** It shares the LOO-PSNR importance definition and the UEP motivation, and it imposes an ordering where we discover the native one. It threatens novelty only for "LOO masking as an importance measure for UEP." It does not threaten the claims that gates fail, that energy and a cheap learned head succeed, or that the result holds across seeds and datasets.
- **Tung, Kurka, Jankowski, Gündüz, "DeepJSCC-Q: Channel Input Constrained Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding," arXiv:2111.13042** [V, existence]. This work uses ADJSCC-style attention / SNR-adaptive layers. A search summary said the attention layer allows adaptive allocation of resources. I did not verify any per-channel importance validation, and none is expected. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.13042). Closeness: related. It uses the same gate family without validation.
- **Choi & Seo, "Feature Importance-Aware Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding for Computationally Efficient and Adjustable Image Transmission," arXiv:2504.04758 (v4 Mar 2026; the page lists a DOI with a JIOT prefix, possibly IEEE IoT Journal)** [V]. FAJSCC uses "selective deformable self-attention," applied only to "selected and adaptively adjusted" important areas, and computes spatial and channel importance through a shared "attention family tree." The importance it uses is attention-derived and serves compute allocation. The abstract does not report validation against reconstruction damage from dropping features. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04758), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04758). Closeness: related. It assumes attention-derived importance is meaningful, which our result questions for the bottleneck.

### Inferences
- Several JSCC papers assume that attention, or a learned score, captures "feature importance." I found none that checks the gates against ground-truth erasure damage. This makes the ADJSCC gate-vs-LOO test (Spearman -0.015) a genuinely missing sanity check in the JSCC literature, analogous to "attention is not explanation" (see Q3).
- Zhan et al. 2026 already uses LOO PSNR as the ground truth. Our paper should cite it, position LOO as an accepted ground truth, and make clear that our contributions are (i) the failure of gates, (ii) cheap proxies that work (energy, a 2048-MAC head), and (iii) seed and dataset stability.

### Gaps
- I could not check whether any IEEE-only JSCC paper (not on arXiv) inspects ADJSCC AF-module scale values per channel. A targeted IEEE Xplore full-text search for "attention feature module" plus "channel importance" would close this gap.
- I could not identify Zhan et al.'s ref [10] exactly. It is likely ADJSCC, but this is unconfirmed.

## Q2. Which semantic communication papers use attention weights or gradients to decide which features to transmit or protect?

### Takeaway
A sizeable line of work uses gradients (Grad-CAM-style), ViT attention scores, learned policy networks or entropy to decide what to send or protect. None of the ones I found validates the score against true per-feature erasure damage. Learned selectors (Yang & Kim; Chen et al.) are conceptually closest to our learned sensitivity head, but they learn a rate mask end to end rather than regressing measured damage.

### Cited Findings
- **Sun, Guo, Yang, et al., "Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding for Wireless Image Transmission with Semantic Importance," arXiv:2302.02287 (SD-JSCC)** [V]. Importance weights come from gradients of the downstream task's output with respect to image features, and they weight the training loss. The paper reports up to 57.7% and 9.1% task-performance gains over SSCC and DJSCC respectively. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.02287). Importance: gradient (task-driven). Validated against damage: no (validated only by end-task gains). Closeness: related.
- **Sun et al., "Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding Based on Semantics of Pixels," arXiv:2208.11375** [V]. This is the same gradient idea applied per pixel and reports a 1.38% classification gain. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.11375). Closeness: related or distant.
- **Yang & Kim, "Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding for Wireless Image Transmission with Adaptive Rate Control," ICASSP 2022, arXiv:2110.04456** [V]. A policy network (Gumbel-Softmax) outputs a thermometer-coded binary mask over latent features, conditioned on SNR and image content, with an efficiency loss. It learns to use less bandwidth at high SNR or for simple images. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04456), [code](https://github.com/mingyuyng/Dynamic_JSCC). Importance: learned, with an imposed ordering. Validated against LOO damage: no. Closeness: related. A learned selector exists, but its ordering is trained in, unlike our post-hoc discovery of a native ordering.
- **Chen, Yang, Chen, Huang, Wang, Xiong, Zhang, "Entropy-and-Channel-Aware Adaptive-Rate Semantic Communication with MLLM-Aided Feature Compensation," arXiv:2501.15414 (v4 Mar 2026)** [V]. Two policy networks use CSI, SNR, the feature maps and their 2D entropy to select a subset of feature maps and symbols. The abstract does not state whether channel-attention weights drive the selection, and I did not see an ablation-based validation. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15414v1). Closeness: related.
- **Prior "passive" importance methods catalogued in Zhan et al. 2026 (refs [23]-[37] there)** [V, via secondary listing]. Grad-CAM, average-gradient methods, knowledge distillation, feature-wise masking, gradient-activation approximation, ViT attention scores, segmentation saliency, embedding cosine similarity, jointly trained evaluation networks, and BER-as-trainable-parameter. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00595v1). This is a useful ready-made taxonomy for our related-work section. The individual papers were not fetched.
- **"Unequal Error Protection for Digital Semantic Communication with Channel Coding," arXiv:2508.03381** [V, existence and snippet]. This paper cites the use of vision-transformer attention scores as patch importance for selection and quantisation. It also treats bit importance via bit-flip probability, with protection learned jointly. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03381); see also the [ResearchGate version](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394323284_Channel_Coding_for_Unequal_Error_Protection_in_Digital_Semantic_Communication). Closeness: related (digital UEP, learned bit importance).
- **"A Technique for Error-resilient Semantic Communication," arXiv:2508.17957** [V, snippet only]. It allocates transmit power by semantic importance, with more power for important packets, and reports gains over DJSCC and JPEG2000. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.17957). Closeness: related. I did not verify how importance is measured.
- **"Reinforcement-Learned Unequal Error Protection for Quantized Semantic Embeddings," arXiv:2601.00186** [V, snippet only]. An RL agent sets per-dimension repetition counts. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00186). Closeness: related (learned per-dimension protection).
- **"Semantic Error Control Coding with Foundation Models...," arXiv:2608.11551** [V, snippet only]. A foundation model estimates per-token importance, and UEP assigns protection accordingly. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11551). Closeness: distant (text and tokens).
- **"Guaranteed Image Classification via Goal-oriented Joint Semantic Source and Channel Coding," arXiv:2603.01872** [V, snippet only]. It gives more compression and stronger protection to semantically important regions. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01872). Closeness: distant (spatial and region-level).
- **Shao, Mao, Zhang, "Learning Task-Oriented Communication for Edge Inference: An Information Bottleneck Approach," IEEE JSAC 2022, arXiv:2102.04170** [K]. Variational feature encoding with a sparsity-inducing prior prunes redundant feature dimensions, so importance is a learned dropout-rate or variance criterion. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04170). Closeness: related (learned dimension importance, task-oriented).
- **Dai, Wang, Tang, et al., "Nonlinear Transform Source-Channel Coding for Semantic Communications," IEEE JSAC 2022, arXiv:2112.10961 (NTSCC)** [K]. A hyperprior entropy model allocates channel bandwidth per latent patch, so importance is estimated entropy (rate) rather than damage. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10961). Closeness: related. Entropy-based allocation is a natural alternative proxy to compare against.
- **Pruning-side analogues of attention-based selection.** AntiDote (Chen et al., arXiv:2008.06543) uses channel-attention scores with a top-k mask for dynamic channel pruning [V] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06543). PCAS (Yamamoto & Maeno, arXiv:1806.05382, 2018; BMVC 2019 [K for venue]) inserts attention modules before target layers of a pretrained network, learns per-channel importance, and prunes using attention statistics over the training data [V] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.05382). CPSCA (Liu, Fang, Ma, Xu, Xiong, Ding, arXiv:2011.03891, 2020) guides pruning with spatial and channel attention and uses an SENet-based pruning baseline ("CPSE") [V] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.03891). Gao et al., "Dynamic Channel Pruning: Feature Boosting and Suppression," ICLR 2019, arXiv:1810.05331 [K], uses an SE-like saliency predictor to skip channels at run time. Closeness: related. These papers use attention or SE as importance, which is the assumption we falsify for JSCC. Note that in PCAS and AntiDote the attention is trained specifically for selection, unlike ADJSCC's gates, which are trained only for SNR adaptation.

### Inferences
- In the literature, "attention as importance" works when the attention is trained for the selection objective (PCAS, AntiDote, FBS, policy networks). Our result concerns gates trained for a different purpose, SNR recalibration, and then read as importance. This distinction should frame our claim: "repurposed SE gates are not importance indicators," not "attention can never encode importance."
- Our 2048-MAC sensitivity head sits between the learned-selector papers (Yang & Kim, Chen et al.) and the LOO ground truth (Zhan et al.). It is a regression-to-damage predictor, which I did not find in JSCC.

### Gaps
- For 2508.17957, 2601.00186, 2603.01872 and 2608.11551 I saw only search snippets, so the importance measures need full-text confirmation before detailed claims.
- The Kang et al. "personalized saliency" JSAC paper and Grad-CAM-based semantic communication papers are mentioned only through the Zhan et al. taxonomy. I did not verify their individual citations.
- For "Task-oriented Explainable Semantic Communications" ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370347549_Task-oriented_Explainable_Semantic_Communications)) I confirmed only the title. Its importance or explainability method was not checked.

## Q3. What established proxies (L1 norm, energy, Taylor expansion, gradient saliency) exist in pruning and compression that relate to our energy and Taylor proxies, and what critiques of attention as explanation exist?

### Takeaway
Our proxies have direct, well-established ancestors. Molchanov et al. 2017 is especially close methodologically: it ranks pruning criteria by Spearman correlation against an "oracle" leave-one-out removal ranking, which is the same evaluation protocol as ours. Molchanov et al. 2019 validates first-order Taylor against true removal importance. L1-norm filter pruning (Li et al. 2017) and BN-scale (network slimming) are the magnitude baselines. The NLP "attention is not explanation" debate (Jain & Wallace; Serrano & Smith; Wiegreffe & Pinter) and the vision "class selectivity is not importance" ablation results (Morcos et al. 2018) are the conceptual precedent for our negative result on gates.

### Cited Findings
Pruning / importance proxies:
- **Li, Kadav, Durdanovic, Samet, Graf, "Pruning Filters for Efficient ConvNets," ICLR 2017, arXiv:1608.08710** [K]. This paper ranks filters by the L1 norm of their weights. Importance is weight magnitude, validated by layer-wise pruning sensitivity and accuracy after pruning. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.08710). Closeness: related (the magnitude-proxy ancestor). Note that it uses weight norm, whereas we use activation and latent energy.
- **Molchanov, Tyree, Karras, Aila, Kautz, "Pruning Convolutional Neural Networks for Resource Efficient Inference," ICLR 2017, arXiv:1611.06440** [K]. This paper proposes a first-order Taylor criterion on feature-map activations (|activation × gradient|). It compares criteria (Taylor, mean activation, activation std, weight-norm style, random, and others) by Spearman rank correlation against an oracle that removes each feature map one at a time and measures the change in loss. Taylor correlates best. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06440). **Closeness: close methodologically.** It uses the same protocol (proxy vs LOO oracle, Spearman), in classifiers rather than JSCC. It does not threaten novelty, but reviewers will expect it to be cited as the protocol precedent.
- **Molchanov, Mallya, Tyree, Frosio, Kautz, "Importance Estimation for Neural Network Pruning," CVPR 2019, arXiv:1906.10771** [K]. This paper defines importance as the squared change in loss when a parameter or filter is removed, approximates it with first- and second-order Taylor expansions (often applied at BN or gate parameters), and reports high correlation with the true removal importance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10771). Closeness: close methodologically, distant in domain.
- **LeCun, Denker, Solla, "Optimal Brain Damage," NeurIPS (NIPS) 1989** [K] (second-order saliency), and **Mozer & Smolensky, "Skeletonization," NIPS 1988** [K] (relevance as the loss change when a unit is removed). These are the historical origin of LOO, Taylor and Hessian importance. — [OBD at NeurIPS proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/1989/hash/6c9882bbac1c7093bd25041881277658-Abstract.html). Closeness: distant background.
- **Liu, Li, Shen, Huang, Yan, Zhang, "Learning Efficient Convolutional Networks through Network Slimming," ICCV 2017, arXiv:1708.06519** [K]. This paper uses L1-regularised BN scaling factors (γ) as channel importance. A multiplicative per-channel scale is structurally similar to an SE gate, but it is trained with sparsity for exactly this purpose. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.06519). Closeness: related.
- **Critiques of norm-based importance:** Ye, Lu, Lin, Wang, "Rethinking the Smaller-Norm-Less-Informative Assumption in Channel Pruning of Convolution Layers," ICLR 2018, arXiv:1802.00124 [K] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00124). He, Liu, Wang, Hu, Yang, "Filter Pruning via Geometric Median (FPGM)," CVPR 2019, arXiv:1811.00250 [K] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00250). Both argue that magnitude is not always a valid importance criterion. Closeness: related. This is a useful counterpoint: our finding that energy works (+0.84) is an empirical property of JSCC bottlenecks under power normalisation and AWGN, not a universal law.

Attention-as-explanation critiques:
- **Jain & Wallace, "Attention is not Explanation," NAACL 2019, arXiv:1902.10186** [K]. Attention weights correlate weakly with gradient- and LOO-based feature importance, and adversarial or permuted attention distributions often leave predictions unchanged. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10186). **Closeness: close conceptually.** It reaches the same kind of conclusion (attention vs LOO importance, weak correlation) in NLP. It is the natural framing reference ("SE gates are not importance").
- **Serrano & Smith, "Is Attention Interpretable?," ACL 2019, arXiv:1906.03731** [K]. Zeroing the highest-attention items often fails to flip decisions, and gradient-based rankings identify decisive items better than attention. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03731). Closeness: close conceptually (an erasure-based test of attention, like ours).
- **Wiegreffe & Pinter, "Attention is not not Explanation," EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019, arXiv:1908.04626** [K]. This paper argues that the conclusion depends on the definition of explanation, and that attention can be meaningful when the model genuinely relies on it (for example, a frozen-attention baseline). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04626). Closeness: related. It is the counter-argument we should pre-empt by showing that the gates do not even give a weak ranking (≈0 Spearman).
- **Morcos, Barrett, Rabinowitz, Botvinick, "On the Importance of Single Directions for Generalization," ICLR 2018, arXiv:1803.06959** [V]. This paper ablates units by clamping them to zero and shows that "class selectivity is a poor predictor of task importance." It also finds that batch norm reduces reliance on single directions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.06959). **Closeness: close conceptually (vision).** An intuitive per-unit statistic fails to predict ablation damage. This is the direct vision analogue of our gate result.
- **Zhou, Sun, Bau, Torralba, "Revisiting the Importance of Individual Units in CNNs via Ablation," arXiv:1806.02891 (2018)** [K]. Ablating single units hurts overall accuracy little but can strongly affect individual classes, and interpretable units matter more for their classes. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02891). Closeness: related.
- **Hu, Shen, Sun, "Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks," CVPR 2018 / TPAMI 2020, arXiv:1709.01507** [V]. Section "Role of Excitation": excitations are class-agnostic in early layers and class-specific deeper. "SE_5_2 exhibits an interesting tendency towards a saturated state in which most of the activations are close to one," which reduces the block to "the identity operator." The last-stage SE blocks "could be removed at only a small cost in performance" (<0.1% top-5). — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.01507), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.01507). Closeness: related. The SE authors themselves interpret excitation as recalibration, not as an importance ranking, and show near-uniform gates in some stages. This is consistent with our finding that bottleneck gates carry little ranking information.
- **Adebayo et al., "Sanity Checks for Saliency Maps," NeurIPS 2018, arXiv:1810.03292** [K], and **Hooker, Erhan, Kindermans, Kim, "A Benchmark for Interpretability Methods in Deep Neural Networks (ROAR)," NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1806.10758** [K]. Both are methodological precedent for validating an importance score by removing features and measuring the actual drop, which is the evaluation our paper uses. — [arXiv 1810.03292](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03292), [arXiv 1806.10758](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10758). Closeness: related.

### Inferences
- Our energy/L1 proxy corresponds to activation-magnitude criteria (activation mean or L1 of the feature map, as in Molchanov 2017's baselines). A Taylor proxy corresponds to Molchanov 2017/2019. The finding that energy works well in a power-normalised AWGN JSCC bottleneck is plausible: under additive noise and erasure-to-zero, the reconstruction damage of erasing a channel scales with how much signal it carries. The Ye et al. 2018 and FPGM critiques imply this is domain-specific rather than general.
- Our paper's evaluation protocol (Spearman and top-k overlap against an LOO oracle) is standard in pruning (Molchanov 2017). Its novelty lies in the domain (JSCC UEP), the specific negative result on SNR-conditioned SE gates, and the seed and dataset stability analysis, not in the protocol itself.

### Gaps
- I did not re-fetch the [K] items this session. The exact numbers in Molchanov 2017 (which criteria and what Spearman values) should be checked before quoting any figures. Only the qualitative claim (Taylor best vs oracle, Spearman protocol) is asserted here.
- I found no vision paper that directly measures SE excitation values against per-channel LOO damage in a classifier. PCAS and CPSCA use attention for pruning, but they do not report correlation with oracle removal. This is a possible broader-novelty angle that is unverified.

## Q4. Is there work on permutation symmetry of channels across independently trained networks (why rankings are checkpoint-specific)?

### Takeaway
Yes. There is a substantial, well-known literature showing that independently trained networks learn similar features up to permutation, or partly different bases, with alignment by bipartite matching. It explains why our per-index rankings differ across seeds while the sorted importance profile is stable (0.92). I found no JSCC- or codec-specific study of this.

### Cited Findings
- **Li, Yosinski, Clune, Lipson, Hopcroft, "Convergent Learning: Do Different Neural Networks Learn the Same Representations?," ICLR 2016, arXiv:1511.07543** [V]. The authors align units across AlexNets trained from different seeds by bipartite matching, one-to-many and many-to-many methods. Some features are learned reliably across networks while the specific basis vectors are not, and units often span shared low-dimensional subspaces. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.07543), [ICLR 2016 listing](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2016/li2016iclr-convergent). Closeness: related (the direct explanation for index permutation across seeds).
- **Entezari, Sedghi, Saukh, Neyshabur, "The Role of Permutation Invariance in Linear Mode Connectivity of Neural Networks," ICLR 2022, arXiv:2110.06296** [K]. This paper conjectures that, once permutation symmetry is accounted for, SGD solutions are linearly connected with no loss barrier. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06296). Closeness: related.
- **Ainsworth, Hayase, Srinivasa, "Git Re-Basin: Merging Models modulo Permutation Symmetries," ICLR 2023, arXiv:2209.04836** [K]. This paper gives practical algorithms (weight matching, activation matching) that permute one network's units to align with another's. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04836). Closeness: related. Activation matching could be used to show that the high-importance channels of seed A map to those of seed B.
- **Kornblith, Norouzi, Lee, Hinton, "Similarity of Neural Network Representations Revisited (CKA)," ICML 2019, arXiv:1905.00414** [K]. CKA is a permutation-invariant representation similarity measure across seeds. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414). Closeness: distant (tooling).

### Inferences
- Our seed result (stable sorted profile, permuted indices) is what the convergent-learning and permutation-symmetry literature predicts. A strong addition would be to align channels across seeds (Hungarian matching on latent activations, as in Li et al. 2016 or Git Re-Basin activation matching) and show that importance ranks agree after alignment. That would turn "checkpoint-specific indices" into "the same features, permuted."
- Because indices permute, any UEP scheme that hard-codes channel indices must be calibrated per checkpoint. This argues for our per-checkpoint learned head or energy proxy over a fixed protection map.

### Gaps
- I found no work on permutation symmetry or seed consistency of latent channels in learned image codecs or Deep JSCC specifically. This is likely an open gap, though I did not search exhaustively.

## Q5. (Objective d) Channel-wise importance or significance in learned image compression

### Takeaway
Learned compression has a well-documented energy compaction or channel ordering phenomenon: early channels carry most energy and entropy (ELIC). Ordering can also be imposed (nested dropout, tail-drop, ProgDTD) to enable progressive coding, and spatial importance maps allocate rate by content (Li et al. 2018). These are the codec-side analogues of our "native importance profile." None validates gate or attention values against erasure damage.

### Cited Findings
- **He, Yang, Peng, Ma, Qin, Wang, "ELIC: Efficient Learned Image Compression with Unevenly Grouped Space-Channel Contextual Adaptive Coding," CVPR 2022, arXiv:2203.10886** [V]. Per-channel energy and entropy in Minnen2018-style latents show that "the beginning channels have much larger average energy," and entropy follows energy (an "information compaction" property). This motivates uneven channel grouping (5 chunks of increasing size). The theory behind the property is left to future work. — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/He_ELIC_Efficient_Learned_Image_Compression_With_Unevenly_Grouped_Space-Channel_Contextual_CVPR_2022_paper.html), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10886). Importance: energy and entropy per channel. Validated against damage: not as LOO, though entropy-energy co-variation is shown. **Closeness: related, and supportive.** It is independent evidence that energy indexes channel significance in learned latents. In ELIC's channel-conditional setting the ordering is induced by the architecture, whereas in ADJSCC we find an unordered native profile.
- **Minnen & Singh, "Channel-wise Autoregressive Entropy Models for Learned Image Compression," ICIP 2020, arXiv:2007.08739** [K]. Channels are split into slices coded sequentially, with later slices conditioned on earlier ones. This leads to the channel-ordering and compaction effects that ELIC analyses. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08739). Closeness: distant or related.
- **Li, Zuo, Gu, Zhao, Zhang, "Learning Convolutional Networks for Content-Weighted Image Compression," CVPR 2018, arXiv:1703.10553** [K]. A learned importance map masks the number of active latent channels (bits) per spatial location, so that more channels go to complex or salient regions. Importance is a learned spatial map under a rate constraint, not validated against LOO damage. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10553). Closeness: related. It is spatial and channel-count importance learned for rate, not post-hoc channel importance.
- **Hojjat, Haberer, Landsiedel, "ProgDTD: Progressive Learned Image Compression with Double-Tail-Drop Training," CVPR Workshops 2023, arXiv:2305.02145** [V]. Starting from the observation that "the information in the bottleneck differs in importance," the paper trains the Ballé hyperprior with tail-drop on both latent and hyper-latent, so that channels are stored in priority order and transmitted sorted. Performance is comparable to non-progressive models in MS-SSIM, with a slight PSNR drop. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02145), [CVPRW listing](https://mlanthology.org/cvprw/2023/hojjat2023cvprw-progdtd). Closeness: related. It imposes an ordering, whereas we measure the native one, and it is the codec analogue of Zhan et al.'s imposed ordering.
- **Rippel, Gelbart, Adams, "Learning Ordered Representations with Nested Dropout," ICML 2014, arXiv:1402.0915** [K]. This is the origin of tail-drop and nested-dropout ordering of latent units. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0915). Closeness: distant background.
- **Kurka & Gündüz, "Bandwidth-Agile Image Transmission with Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding," IEEE TWC 2021, arXiv:2009.12480** [K]. Successive-refinement DeepJSCC trains layered or ordered latents so that prefixes are decodable. This is the JSCC version of imposed ordering. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12480). Closeness: related.

### Inferences
- There are two camps. The first imposes importance ordering through training (nested dropout, tail-drop, ProgDTD, successive-refinement DeepJSCC, Yang & Kim thermometer masks, Zhan et al. ISFR). The second observes ordering through energy and entropy statistics (ELIC). Our contribution sits in the second camp for Deep JSCC: it measures the native, unordered importance profile of a standard ADJSCC, shows that energy predicts it while the built-in gates do not, and provides a cheap learned predictor. This lets UEP be applied to off-the-shelf models without retraining for an ordering.
- ELIC's energy-entropy compaction observation gives independent support that energy is a meaningful channel-significance statistic in learned latents. It can be cited alongside our +0.84 energy-LOO correlation.

### Gaps
- I did not find any learned-compression paper that measures per-channel LOO (erasure) damage and correlates it with energy. ELIC reports energy-entropy co-variation, not energy-vs-damage. This appears to be a gap our work fills for JSCC, but I did not search exhaustively.
- I did not verify cross-dataset transfer of channel importance profiles (our CIFAR-10 to CIFAR-100 result, Spearman 0.997) in any prior codec or JSCC work. I found no precedent.

### Overall novelty assessment (inference, drawn from all sections above)
- **Near-duplicate:** none found.
- **Close:** Zhan et al. 2026 (arXiv:2604.00595), which uses the same LOO-PSNR importance for UEP in semantic communication. Molchanov et al. 2017 shares the same proxy-vs-oracle Spearman protocol, in pruning. Jain & Wallace 2019, Serrano & Smith 2019 and Morcos et al. 2018 reach the same conceptual result (an intuitive score fails to predict ablation damage) in NLP and vision classifiers.
- **Contributions that appear novel:** (1) SNR-conditioned SE / AF gates in ADJSCC do not predict erasure damage (≈0 Spearman, chance top-k). (2) Latent energy and a tiny learned sensitivity head do predict it, in a JSCC UEP setting. (3) Importance profiles are stable across seeds up to permutation and transfer across datasets in JSCC.
- **Positioning risk:** reviewers may say "attention trained for X need not encode Y is known" (Jain & Wallace, Morcos et al.). The defence is that JSCC papers (ADJSCC, FAJSCC, semantic-communication attention-selection works) explicitly or implicitly interpret attention as feature importance, that nobody had tested this, and that the test has practical consequences for UEP.

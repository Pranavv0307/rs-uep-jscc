# Measured sensitivity, not tiered parity, carries the novelty

No prior paper found in this review combines the paper's full recipe: a byte-quantized ADJSCC latent, per-channel leave-one-out (LOO) erasure sensitivity, tiered Reed-Solomon (RS) parity at a fixed overhead, i.i.d. and Gilbert-Elliott packet erasures, and JPEG/JPEG2000/WebP + RS baselines. Most of the individual pieces have been published before, though, so the defensible novelty is in the *measurements*, not the coding machinery. The closest prior work is **Grad-FEC** (Uyanik, Yeganli, Bajić, ICIP 2023). It protects importance-ranked, 8-bit deep-feature packets with systematic RS erasure codes under i.i.d. packet loss, but for split-computing classification rather than JSCC reconstruction ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01846)). **ISFR** (Zhan et al., 2026) already uses LOO-PSNR masking as a feature-importance measure for UEP, over bit-error/fading channels and without FEC ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00595)). **Priority Encoding Transmission** (Albanese et al., 1996) and **Unequal Loss Protection** (Mohr, Riskin, Ladner, 2000) are the origin of unequal RS protection across packets ([PET](https://ris.uni-paderborn.de/record/3034); [ULP](https://researchwith.stevens.edu/en/publications/unequal-loss-protection-graceful-degradation-of-image-quality-ove/)). Three claims have no precedent in any of the six literatures reviewed. First, ADJSCC's SNR-conditioned attention gates do not predict erasure damage (Spearman −0.015, chance-level top-4 overlap). Second, latent energy (Spearman +0.84) and a 2048-MAC head (top-4 overlap 0.92) do predict it, and they turn into UEP gains that match or beat the oracle ranking. Third, the sorted sensitivity profile is stable across seeds while channel indices permute, and it transfers from CIFAR-10 to CIFAR-100. Reviewers will notice that our grouped-tier RS layout is a relative of PET/ULP. Each of our tiers owns its own packets, whereas in PET all tiers share all packets. They will also notice that the loss below ~32% follows from splitting the parity and shortening the blocks relative to one RS(96,64) code. The paper should therefore say this itself, add a PET shared-packet twin and an optimal-parity search as baselines, add BPG + RS and a Grad-FEC-style drop-and-protect scheme, and anonymize the public GitHub repository before submission.

## How to read the evidence tables

Every area below closes with a table of the works the research notes found. **Closeness** uses the scale requested: near-duplicate / close / related / distant. **Threat** says which of our claims a work weakens (High / Medium / Low). The **Verif.** column carries the notes' own verification status, and anything other than V needs a check before it goes into the paper. **V** means the paper page or full text was checked. **A** means only an abstract, search snippet or indexed record was seen, so details beyond the abstract are unconfirmed. **K** means a canonical paper that the note-taker cited from prior knowledge without re-opening the link this session. **U** means unverified: the paper came from another paper's reference list or from memory, or its metadata conflicts across sources. **None of the roughly 120 works reviewed is a near-duplicate.** Where the notes disagree with each other, this report says so and states which version it follows.

## Six literatures converge on our design point but none occupies it

### Importance-aware protection of learned latents: Grad-FEC is the one close rival

The first literature is unequal or importance-aware protection of Deep JSCC and semantic-communication latents. Most of this field allocates *physical-layer* resources (power, subcarriers, quantization bits, constellation points) over AWGN or fading channels, not packet-level FEC. Only two lines put block codes on quantized learned features. **Grad-FEC** cuts a ResNet-50 at conv2_block1_add and quantizes the 56×56×256 tensor to 8 bits. Each packet holds 7 rows of one channel. Packets are ranked with a modified Grad-CAM map, which a proxy CNN imitates so the ranking can be computed at the edge. The scheme FEC_A_B then drops the A% least important packets and spends the freed slots on systematic RS parity for the most important B%. It reports "near-lossless performance even with 50% packet loss" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.01846)). The notes could not confirm Grad-FEC's exact RS (n,k) values. They also found no equal-overhead equal-protection baseline in its results, and its validation of importance is a coarse most-versus-least drop test, not a per-unit LOO measurement ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.01846)). **Kim, Oh, Kim, Lee and Jeon** quantize a DeepJSCC-style autoencoder to 8 bits per feature and learn per-bit flip tolerances that differ "by orders of magnitude". They then protect bit groups with repetition codes or short 5G polar/LDPC blocks, with a finite-blocklength threshold for when partitioning pays off. Their channel is Rayleigh block fading plus AWGN, with no erasures ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03381)). Their finite-blocklength partitioning result bears directly on our low-loss penalty, so it should be cited in the RS-layout discussion as well as in related work.

The remaining works protect by other means. Some use rateless expanding-window codes over discrete tokens, as in **TokCom-UEP** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22859)). Others map packets to priority streams of a multi-level erasure channel with no FEC, as in the Nokia DCVC-RT codec ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16279)), or allocate power by semantic importance ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17957); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.07142v2)). Bajić, Lin and Tian's collaborative-intelligence survey explicitly proposed attention as a feature-importance measure and "varying-strength channel codes" as the protection ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.06841)). That makes it a useful foil for our negative result on gates. Singh and Arif argue that block codes such as LDPC or RS cannot give per-dimension protection ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00186)), and our channel-granular RS tiers are a working counterexample at the granularity that matters.

### Packet-erasure Deep JSCC is a 2025–2026 boom, and nobody puts a real RS code on the latent

The second literature, Deep JSCC under packet erasure, agrees across papers. An erasure-trained network degrades gracefully, an erasure-naive one collapses, and codec + FEC collapses past its design loss rate. **Esfahanizadeh, Fayaz, Du and Viswanathan** (Nokia Bell Labs) train a JSCC autoencoder over independent block erasures. They fill erased blocks with a −1 sentinel, quantize each latent value to **8 bits at inference "which preserves performance"**, and get *implicit* UEP by training with a non-uniform erasure vector. They use no FEC of any kind and do not compare against JPEG, BPG or WebP ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20707)). **LGSemCom** (Bo et al.) already evaluates i.i.d. and 3-state Markov bursty loss (PLR 13.8% and 32.3%). It replaces missing bits with a learnable mask token and compares against JPEG AI, Cheng2020, BPG and JPEG2000 with *ideal* packet FEC at 12.5–87.5% parity, which "degrade rapidly" with "quality degradation or total collapse" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06989)). This rules out claiming to be first with bursty loss, first with erasure-aware training, or first with latent inpainting. Our distinct angle is narrower and still open: **a real RS code over a quantized JSCC latent brings its own block-level cliff into a JSCC system**, and we ask whether importance-tiered parity, zero-fill and fine-tuning turn that cliff into a slope.

Digital Deep JSCC with conventional channel codes works almost entirely at the physical layer. DeepJSCC-Q, MDJCM, VQ-DeepSC and masked VQ-VAE quantize onto constellations or codebooks over AWGN or fading and benchmark against **BPG + LDPC**, not codec + RS over packets ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08100); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05437v2)). The notes flag that the standard separation baseline in this lineage is BPG (or VTM/JPEG AI) with LDPC or ideal FEC. Our JPEG/JPEG2000/WebP + RS set is defensible but will draw a "where is BPG?" comment. The repository log confirms BPG was planned in the roadmap and skipped only because `bpgenc` was not installed. Two mechanistic results are useful to us. **Yang, Ma, Liang and Shi** find that CNN JSCC concentrates information unevenly across channels and degrades badly when dominant channels are lost, while channel-partitioned Transformers are intrinsically robust ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16983)). That explains why our ADJSCC latent has an exploitable skew, but it also invites a "would SwinJSCC remove the gain?" question. **Every Packet Counts** (ACM MM 2026) reports, according to search snippets of its full text, that channel-wise packet loss in learned image codecs causes very inconsistent PSNR drops driven by imbalanced latent-channel energy ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11096)). That independently supports our energy proxy, but the paper's response is the opposite of ours: it *disperses* energy across packets instead of protecting it unequally.

### Attention as an importance signal: an untested assumption in JSCC, a settled debate elsewhere

The third literature concerns which latent channels matter and whether attention reveals it. Here our negative result is genuinely missing from JSCC. ADJSCC's attention-feature module pools features globally, concatenates the SNR, and outputs per-channel scale factors. Its stated rationale is that the network learns "to assign different weights to different features for a given SNR", and the paper never checks that rationale against per-channel damage ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351791034_Wireless_Image_Transmission_Using_Deep_Source_Channel_Coding_With_Attention_Modules)). FAJSCC, the ViT-importance papers and the Bajić survey all treat attention-derived scores as importance ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04758); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06038)). **No paper in the notes checks SE/attention gates against LOO erasure damage**, so our within-image Spearman of −0.015 fills a real gap. The *measurement* is not new, however. **ISFR defines feature importance as the PSNR drop when one feature is zeroed, normalized to sum to one**, and stores it as a prior for UEP ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00595v1)). ISFR also catalogues earlier "passive" importance methods (Grad-CAM, gradients, feature masking, ViT attention, learned evaluators), which gives a ready-made related-work taxonomy. Our evaluation protocol, ranking a proxy against a leave-one-out oracle by Spearman correlation, goes back to **Molchanov et al. (ICLR 2017)**, where first-order Taylor beat activation and weight-norm criteria ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06440), K).

The conceptual precedent for "a plausible per-unit score fails to predict ablation damage" is strong, and reviewers will raise it. **Jain and Wallace** show attention correlates weakly with gradient and LOO importance ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10186), K). **Serrano and Smith** show that zeroing the highest-attention items often fails to flip decisions ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03731), K). **Morcos et al.** show that "class selectivity is a poor predictor of task importance" under unit ablation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.06959)). The SE paper itself reports that late-stage excitations saturate near one and act as "the identity operator" ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.01507)). The notes' key framing advice follows from this. Attention works as importance in pruning when it is *trained for selection* (PCAS, AntiDote, FBS). ADJSCC's gates were trained for SNR recalibration and are then *repurposed* as importance. The defensible claim is therefore "repurposed SNR gates are not erasure-importance indicators," not "attention never encodes importance." Wiegreffe and Pinter's "attention is not not explanation" counterargument ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04626), K) is best met by pointing out that our gates show no ranking signal at all (≈0 Spearman, chance top-4).

Energy has supportive precedents but also known limits. **ELIC** shows that "the beginning channels have much larger average energy" in learned-compression latents and that entropy follows energy ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/He_ELIC_Efficient_Learned_Image_Compression_With_Unevenly_Grouped_Space-Channel_Contextual_CVPR_2022_paper.html)). Magnitude-based pruning criteria (L1 filter pruning, network slimming) are its ancestors, and Ye et al. and FPGM argue that magnitude is not always a valid criterion ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00124); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00250), K). The paper should present +0.84 as an empirical property of a power-normalized, zero-filled JSCC bottleneck, not as a general law. The seed-permutation result is what convergent-learning work predicts: **independently trained networks learn shared features in different bases**, which can be aligned by bipartite matching ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.07543); Git Re-Basin [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04836), K). The notes found no JSCC or codec study of seed stability or cross-dataset transfer of channel-importance profiles, so our 0.92 seed and 0.997 transfer figures have no precedent.

### Classic UEP: our layout is grouped ULP, a relative of PET, and that explains the low-loss penalty

The fourth literature is classic UEP for layered codecs. It makes unequal RS protection over packets a 30-year-old idea, so the *layout* cannot be a contribution. **PET** gives each message segment a "priority" equal to the fraction of packets needed to recover it, erasure-codes every segment across *all* packets, and proves that no system implements a set of priorities at total rate above one ([Paderborn record](https://ris.uni-paderborn.de/record/3034)). **ULP** applies this to SPIHT so quality degrades gradually. Lenna optimized for 20% mean loss keeps reasonable quality at 40% loss ([Stevens record](https://researchwith.stevens.edu/en/publications/unequal-loss-protection-graceful-degradation-of-image-quality-ove/)). Allocation optimizers followed: Chande–Farvardin's DP, Stankovic–Hamzaoui's linear-time searches with systematic RS across packets, and Dumitrescu–Wu–Wang's **globally optimal UEP packetization in O(N²L²)** for any loss PMF ([author PDF](https://www.ece.mcmaster.ca/~sorina/papers/UEP_TMM_last.pdf)). Hamzaoui, Stankovic and Xiong's *Signal Processing Magazine* survey is the best single citation for the whole line ([Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/36506/)). When a source has no embedded order, the classic answer is to measure distortion-if-lost per unit (H.264 slices, FMO groups) and allocate FEC by RD optimization. Goshi et al. instead reorder H.263 to *make* it embedded ([Stevens record](https://researchwith.stevens.edu/en/publications/unequal-loss-protection-for-h263-compressed-video/); [NYCU](https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/handle/11536/5495)). Our "sort latent channels by measured sensitivity, then tier" is the learned-latent version of that move.

**One research note gets our layout wrong, and the paper must not repeat the error.** The classic-UEP note says our layout "is the same construction as PET". The code says otherwise. In `coding/rs_pipeline.py`, each tier is its own block, "every packet belongs to exactly one tier", and tiers go back to back. The selected `three_tier_25_50_25` scheme is RS(32,16) for the 4 most sensitive channels, RS(48,32) for the middle 8, and RS(16,16), meaning no parity, for the last 4, against uniform RS(96,64). This is **grouped (partitioned) tiered packet FEC**: related to ULP/PET in spirit, but structurally different in four ways. The first difference is the decoding event. A PET tier with code rate k/96 decodes iff at most 96−k of *all* packets are lost, so the number of lost packets alone fixes a nested staircase of recovered tiers. A grouped tier decodes iff at most n_i−k_i of *its own* n_i packets are lost, so tier failures are independent. The second is block length. Every PET codeword has length 96, while ours have length 32 or 48. The third is burst behaviour. Every PET packet carries a slice of every tier, so a burst hits all tiers proportionally, whereas our layout needed explicit timeline interleaving. Our own Gilbert-Elliott results show that sending tiers in order loses the gain (35% loss, burst 16: −0.56 dB in order, +0.80 dB interleaved). The fourth is granularity. PET can be written per byte column: each of the 16 columns is an RS(96,k_j) codeword with Σk_j = 1024 bytes of data. Any per-channel rate profile is then possible, while ours must align tiers to whole groups of four packets.

The low-loss penalty therefore has **two separable causes**, and the paper should name both instead of calling it "a block-length effect" alone. The first is **parity splitting**, which PET shares and which follows from its rate-one bound. Concentrating parity on tier 0 leaves the last tier with none, so at 20% loss about 20% of its packets are simply gone, while RS(96,64) almost never fails. The second is **finite block length**, which only the grouped layout has. Under i.i.d. loss the binomial tail of a shorter MDS code is less steep (our derivation from the MDS decoding rule, not from the notes). RS(48,32) fails with probability **0.93% at 20% loss versus 0.07% for RS(96,64)**, and 25.1% versus 20.4% at 30%. **Above the threshold the order reverses**: at 40% loss RS(48,32) fails 78.6% of the time and RS(96,64) 89.1%. Part of our high-loss "UEP gain" is therefore a short-block effect, not an importance effect. The repository already separates the two. Random-order tiers gain only **+0.50 dB** against **+2.19 dB** for the oracle ranking (mean over loss ≥ 0.35), so about three-quarters of the gain comes from *which* channels are protected. The candidate `two_tier_50_50`, which is two RS(48,32) blocks at exactly the uniform code rate, isolates pure block length and should be reported against uniform in the paper. This framing matches the finite-blocklength literature ([Polyanskiy–Poor–Verdú PDF](https://www.math.chalmers.se/~hegarty/finite_block.pdf)), the short-block RS analysis in [EPFL ICME 2008](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/lts4/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/icme2008b.pdf), and Kim et al.'s finite-blocklength partitioning threshold ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03381)). The notes found no classic paper that quantifies "UEP versus one large EEP block, below versus above threshold" head to head, so a short analytical treatment would be a small genuine contribution.

**A PET shared-packet layout and an optimal parity search are the baselines reviewers will most want, and both are cheap.** Every grouped scheme has a same-rate PET twin. Tier i, with n_i packets in our layout, maps to n_i/6 byte-columns at rate k_i/n_i over all 96 packets, which sums to exactly 16 columns. Comparing twins isolates block length from parity allocation. The table above shows the result is not obvious in advance. The PET twin protects the top tier far better at 40% loss (1.8% versus 9.2% failure) but protects the middle tier worse (89% versus 79%). For the search, the per-column PET form turns the problem into choosing 16 integers k_j ≤ 96 with Σk_j = 1024, maximizing expected PSNR under a loss PMF. Because our tiers' utility adds up across channels, rather than depending on correct prefixes as in SPIHT, the problem is close to a separable knapsack. Exhaustive search over a coarse grid is trivial, and Dumitrescu et al. can be cited for global optimality. The search should be run (a) per known loss rate, where it can never do worse than EEP because EEP is in the search space, and (b) for a loss PMF or with mismatch, which is the honest robustness test. A fixed scheme loses below ~32%, but a loss-rate-aware selector over {uniform, tiered} would give max(EEP, UEP). The paper should state what loss knowledge such a selector assumes. Reviewers may also ask "why RS and not fountain codes?". The notes supply the answer: at short blocks RS is exactly MDS, GF(256) symbols match byte-quantized latents, and rateless UEP has finite-length overhead ([Rahnavard et al.](https://hdl.handle.net/1853/26673); [EWF](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/expanding-window-fountain-codes-for-unequal-error-protection/)). Layer-aware FEC, where enhancement-layer parity also repairs the base layer, is the natural future-work fix for the parity-split penalty ([RiuNet](https://riunet.upv.es/handle/10251/56298?show=full)).

### Speech: Glaris sits closest, and AMR bit classes are the oldest precedent

The fifth literature, speech semantic communication and neural speech codecs under packet loss, is crowded and mostly comes from one group. Three closely related papers (DSST, SoundSpring, Glaris) come from the BUPT Dai/Niu/Zhang group, so that group is the likely reviewer pool. **Glaris** (Han, Dai, Yao et al., Dec 2025) uses the same LibriSpeech 16 kHz setup and the same packet-erasure abstraction. It reuses hyperprior side information z as in-band FEC (N backup copies at non-consecutive offsets), conceals with learned mask tokens, and evaluates i.i.d., 3-state Markov, INTERSPEECH 2022 PLC traces and 5G HARQ traces. It reports **PESQ 2.63 vs 2.14 for Opus DRED at 18 kbps and 30% loss**, with no RS and no importance ranking inside the main latent ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08203)). **DeepSC-S** already uses SE attention in speech JSCC ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12605)), so a with/without-SE ablation is not new by itself. **DSST** already allocates rate by hyperprior-estimated per-frame importance and compares against Opus and AMR-WB + 5G LDPC ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.02283)). The oldest precedent is standardized. **3GPP AMR sorts speech bits into Classes A/B/C by subjective importance**, with a CRC and the strongest protection on Class A ([3GPP SP-99253](https://3gpp.org/FTP/tsg_sa/TSG_SA/TSGS_04/Docs/PDF/SP-99253.pdf)). Our speech extension should be framed as a learned, erasure-domain analogue of AMR bit classes. AMR's classes protect against bit errors, while EVS channel-aware mode, Opus LBRR, DRED and Glaris are the packet-erasure lineage ([EUSIPCO 2018](https://new.eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2018/papers/1570437225.pdf)).

The planned "Opus + RS" baseline will look like a **straw man** if Opus's own loss tools are switched off. Every recent packet-loss speech paper compares against Opus 1.5 with LBRR/DRED and neural PLC, plus at least one RVQ neural codec ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.04453v3); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08203)). RVQ levels are importance-ordered by construction, so "why not UEP over RVQ levels of EnCodec at 6 kbps?" is the obvious reviewer question. Alignment with Glaris also calls for WER, a non-intrusive MOS (DNSMOS/PLCMOS), a tail metric (PESQ-Q10) and real traces or Markov bursts. Our 1.024 s segment implies about 1 s of algorithmic delay, against Glaris's 20 ms causal frames. The speech section should therefore be framed as streaming or broadcast, not conversational.

### Ordered and multiple-description latents bracket our design point from both sides

The sixth literature, progressive, ordered, nested-dropout and multiple-description representations, splits into two opposing camps, and our paper occupies the gap between them. The **imposed-ordering** camp trains importance in. It includes nested dropout ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0915), K), TailDrop's stochastic bottleneck ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.02870)), DeepJSCC-l successive refinement ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12480)), ProgDTD ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02145)), Esfahanizadeh's increasing erasure vector, and ISFR's nested dropout plus BER matching. The **equalizing** camp removes importance. It includes DeepJSCC-l's multiple-description mode, uniform-erasure training, and Every Packet Counts' dispersal. Our approach **exploits the skew that emerges without being trained for, using classical RS and no retraining**. The notes offer a strong rebuttal to "just train it ordered". **ISFR trains with nested dropout and still measures per-feature LOO importance afterwards** ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00595)). Ordering fixes the *rank* of importance but not its *magnitude*, and parity allocation needs magnitudes. The reported cost of imposing order is small but real. ISFR is about 1.3% below the best unordered baseline at high SNR. The DeepJSCC-l MD full decoder "stays below a joint decoder trained only for the full bandwidth" (on Kodak with two layers, 26.85 dB from both layers against about 25 dB from either alone). ProgDTD loses slightly in PSNR ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.12480)). No paper reports the clean ablation of the same backbone with and without ordering under the same RS UEP over packet erasure. That makes it an experiment we can own rather than a threat.

The repository already covers more of the reviewer checklist than most preprints in this space. All image phases ran on Kaggle GPUs with five converged seeds, validation-only scheme selection, a once-per-table test protocol, and 95% CIs of about ±0.03–0.07 dB. The runs include the attention-versus-damage test, CIFAR-100 transfer, cheap proxies, the sensitivity head, erasure-aware decoder training and the inpainting head (partial success), Kodak at 768×512 (CIFAR-trained, clean PSNR 24.3 dB), a bandwidth-ratio sweep, Gilbert-Elliott bursts with interleaving, and JPEG/JPEG2000/WebP + RS. The burst gains (0.5–2.5 dB) missed the roadmap's >3 dB target. Two runs did not converge and are flagged rather than hidden. The **DeepJSCC-Q no-attention ablation (G0/G1) and the LibriSpeech extension (S1) are written but not run**, BPG has not been run, and the manuscript has not been started. The DeepJSCC-Q ablation matters more than its "planned" label suggests. The DeepJSCC-Q conference paper reportedly uses ADJSCC-style SNR attention itself ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.13042)). Without the ablation, a reviewer can argue that both the gate failure and the energy result are quirks of one backbone. The ablation also tests whether energy remains predictive once the gates are gone.

**Positioning.** The current working title, *When Attention Fails*, is a good hook, but the body must frame the result as repurposed gates, not attention in general, or the Jain-and-Wallace reviewer will call it known. The abstract should open with the measurement result and the practical consequence: a transmitter can rank channels by energy or a negligible-cost head, the ranking needs calibrating only once per checkpoint, and it transfers across datasets. The tiered-RS system then serves as the testbed. The introduction should state explicitly that the layout is a grouped-tier relative of PET/ULP. It should also present the low-loss penalty as a characterized trade-off with two named causes and a PET-twin comparison, not as a weakness to hide. Grad-FEC is best framed as the split-inference counterpart, and our work as the reconstruction-JSCC counterpart with four advances: an LOO ground truth, multi-tier parity against a matched-overhead EEP baseline, a crossover analysis, and the attention test. The design-space taxonomy separates the three camps: imposed ordering, equalization/dispersal, and exploiting emergent skew. It is the cleanest answer to "why not nested dropout?" and "why not interleave like Every Packet Counts?". The likely reviewer pool is BUPT (Dai/Niu/Zhang), Nokia Bell Labs (Esfahanizadeh/Viswanathan), SFU (Bajić) and Imperial (Gündüz), so their works above must be cited accurately.

## Practical risks before submission

**Anonymity is the most urgent risk.** A web search for "deep JSCC packet erasure Reed-Solomon" returns the project repository (github.com/Subhashhari/rs-uep-jscc) and a fork (github.com/Pranavv0307/rs-uep-jscc), titled "Investigating importance-aware Reed–Solomon coding for Deep JSCC over packet-erasure channels" ([GitHub](https://github.com/Subhashhari/rs-uep-jscc)). The README also carries the working title *When Attention Fails* and names the target venues. If the GLOBECOM/ICC call requires double-blind review (check the current CFP, since ComSoc policies vary by year), a reviewer searching the title or key phrases will find the authors. Making the repository private does not hide the fork: when a public repository goes private, GitHub detaches its existing public forks, and they stay public. The fork owner must also act. The safest sequence is: ask the fork owner to delete or privatize the fork, make the main repository private for the review window, provide code through an anonymized mirror (for example anonymous.4open.science), use a paper title that differs from the README's, keep any public Weights & Biases project private, and check the CFP's rules on arXiv preprints before posting one.

**Technical exposure reviewers will probe.** First, the per-image channel ranking is 16 bytes of side information that `rs_pipeline.py` "currently assume[s] to be delivered reliably". Either count it and place it in the strongest tier, or use the static per-checkpoint ranking that the transfer result justifies. Second, Kodak results come from a CIFAR-trained model at 24.3 dB clean PSNR, so only relative behaviour can be claimed. Third, the backbone question: Yang et al. suggest Transformers lack the channel skew we exploit. Fourth, zero-fill versus the −1 sentinel and learnable mask tokens used by competitors. Fifth, the speech extension's 1 s latency. Sixth, the 2025–2026 preprint flood: Glaris, ISFR, Esfahanizadeh, LGSemCom and Every Packet Counts all appeared within a year, so the related work should be re-checked against arXiv shortly before submission. Seventh, several citations below rest on secondary references. The notes could not open full texts for TokCom-UEP, Mortaheb, Fayaz or Zhan beyond abstracts, could not confirm Grad-FEC's RS parameters, and did not search IEEE Xplore full text, so 2026 conference versions could be missing.

## Conclusion

The literature changes what the paper is about. The tiered-RS UEP system, which was the intended contribution, is the least novel part, since PET, ULP and Grad-FEC cover nearly all of its machinery. What no one has done is check whether a Deep JSCC model's own attention says which latent channels matter under erasure, and then show that a nearly free statistic and a 2048-MAC head do. That finding has practical reach beyond this paper: any UEP, packet-scheduling or rate-allocation scheme that reads importance off SE-style gates is building on an untested signal. The low-loss penalty, which looks like a weakness, becomes an asset once it is decomposed. The grouped layout pays a finite-block-length tax below the EEP threshold and collects a short-block bonus above it, and the PET twin and optimal search show how much of each is structural and how much comes from importance.

Strategically, the paper should run the cheap coding-theory controls (PET twin, optimal search, block-length decomposition) and BPG + RS before spending compute on speech. Those controls answer the reviewers most likely to sit on a GLOBECOM/ICC committee, while the speech extension enters a field where Glaris has already set the baseline bar at Opus DRED plus RVQ codecs. The DeepJSCC-Q no-attention ablation is the one planned experiment that directly protects the headline claim, so it should run first. The repository and its fork should be anonymized before any submission is uploaded.
